Lesson 1: Understanding Income, Expenses and Profit

Discover the difference between the money your business receives, the money it spends and the profit it earns.

Understanding Your Business Money

Every business owner needs to understand three important financial terms: revenue, expenses and profit.

1. Revenue

Revenue is the money your business earns from selling products or services. For example, if you sell products worth P15,000 during a month, your sales revenue is P15,000.

2. Expenses

Expenses are the costs of operating your business. These may include stock, rent, transport, electricity, wages and other business costs.

3. Profit

Profit is what remains after subtracting the relevant expenses from revenue. Your final profit may also be affected by taxes, interest and other costs.

Practical Example

Imagine your business has the following monthly figures:

Sales revenue: P15,000

Total listed expenses: P9,000

Profit before other applicable costs: P6,000

A Lesson Every Entrepreneur Must Remember

Sales revenue is not the same as profit, and profit is not necessarily the same as cash available to withdraw. Your business may still need money for upcoming bills, stock, taxes, debt repayments and emergencies.

Record your income and expenses regularly. Review your financial position before deciding how much money you can safely spend, withdraw or reinvest.
